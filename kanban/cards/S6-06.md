## Notes

verify.sh step 9 recomputes the same HMAC externally.

**The signature is over the bytes, not the struct.** The body is marshalled once per
event and the same slice is signed and sent on every attempt —
`TestTheSameBytesAreSignedOnEveryAttempt` asserts that all three attempts carry
identical bodies *and* identical signatures. A re-marshal per attempt would put a
retry at the mercy of any field whose encoding ever moved. The test recomputes the
HMAC from the raw bytes the receiver read, which is what `verify.sh` step 9 does from
outside the process.

**"3 attempts, 2 s / 10 s / 60 s" is read as three attempts *at* those offsets from
the close**, which is the only reading that uses all three numbers with three
attempts, and it is how the card's deliverable words it ("attempts at 2 s / 10 s /
60 s"). The offsets are measured from the trigger rather than from the previous
attempt, so a receiver that answers at 9 s still gets its second attempt at the 10 s
mark; the fake-clock test asserts the sleeps are 2 s, 8 s and 50 s, which is what
"at 2/10/60" looks like from inside the loop.

**No permanent-failure fast path, unlike the upload queue.** A receiver's 4xx is
often a proxy answering while the real service restarts, and the whole ladder spans
72 seconds. The upload queue's 20 attempts over four and a half days is where stopping
early mattered. Every attempt's status code and error are recorded, so "it answered
400 three times" is readable without the loop having guessed.

**The signing secret is sealed, so the deliverer has to open it before signing.**
S7-02 sealed every credential this server stores and a webhook's signing secret is
one; signing the sealed text would produce a signature no receiver can reproduce, and
the failure would look like the receiver's. The three ways that fails are answered
differently because the operator's fix differs: an empty secret sends **no header**
(a signature under an empty key looks like one that means something); a value that is
not a sealed value at all is used as written (a row from before sealing, or one
inserted by hand); a well-formed sealed value this process cannot open —
`NVR_SECRET_KEY` changed — also sends no header, because a *bad* signature sends the
operator to the endpoint instead of to the key.

**Delivery is asynchronous and the row is written when the attempt ends.** A close is
applied inside the worker's request; an endpoint that is down would otherwise hold it
open for the ladder. `NotifyClose` resolves the endpoints on the caller's goroutine
(one indexed query, so a disabled endpoint sends nothing rather than "nothing after a
goroutine starts") and hands each target a goroutine. A row is written after the
attempt rather than before: the alternative is a `pending` row nobody will ever close
out, and the history is read by someone who wants answers, not intentions. A process
that shuts down between attempts therefore records the sends it made and nothing
else.

**The payload's two URLs needed a configuration value the plan does not name.**
`PUBLIC_BASE_URL` is the origin a *receiver* can dial. The bind address (`:8080`)
names no host; the `Host` header of the worker's POST is the name the *worker* dials,
which inside compose is `server:8080` and useless to automation outside that network;
and `GO2RTC_PUBLIC_URL` is a different process on a different port. Empty falls back
to `http://localhost:8080` and the server logs that once at boot. A base with a path
prefix is preserved, so a reverse proxy at `/nvr` hands out URLs its receivers can
fetch. Documented in `docs/configuration.md` as a deviation from the plan's table.

**`snapshot_url` and `clip_url` are asymmetric on purpose.** The picture is written on
the open, so by the close an empty `snapshot_url` is a fact and the receiver branches
on the field instead of fetching a 404. The clip is assembled *after* the close, so
its URL is an address that becomes a video a few seconds later — except for a camera
in `record_mode=off`, which will never have one, and there the field is empty.

**Wired into the signal path, not just built.** `handlers.Webhooks` is set in
`main.go` in the plan's boot order, and `applySignal` calls `NotifyClose` when the
applied action is `close` — open and extend send nothing, because an event still
moving is not a fact yet. `server/api/webhooks_wiring_test.go` proves it over the
route the worker posts to, with the real deliverer rather than a spy, so the check
that the bytes which leave the process still verify spans both packages. Shutdown
gives an in-flight delivery 5 s and then stops the ladder.

**Zones carry names, not ids.** The row stores ids because the worker's signal carries
ids; a receiver has never seen them. The camera row holds the pair, which is why
`NotifyClose` is handed the camera as well as the event, and why the payload builder
resolves them the same way the API's event view does. A deleted camera degrades the
payload (no slug, ids where names were) rather than losing the notification: the event
is the fact.
