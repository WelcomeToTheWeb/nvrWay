# S5-05 — zones.py: point-in-polygon, bottom-center centroid, intrusion + loiter

## Deliverable

Ray-cast point_in_polygon; centroid = ((x1+x2)/2, y2) normalized by frame size (feet, not torso); intrusion fires on a matched class inside the polygon at conf >= zone.min_confidence; loiter fires when the same track_id stays inside for >= dwell_seconds (exit resets).

## Exit criteria

- [x] concave polygon, on-edge and outside cases asserted
- [x] box [100,50,140,150] on 640x360 -> (0.1875, 0.41667) — the plan's example x is a slip, see Notes
- [x] loiter fires only after dwell_seconds of continuous presence; leaving resets the timer

## Verification

`cd detector && python3 -m pytest tests/test_zones.py -q`

**Observed** 2026-11-03, in `nvr-detector:dev`: `19 passed in 0.03s`.

- **Concave, on-edge, outside.** The polygon is a U — the unit square minus
  `x∈[0.3,0.7], y∈[0.3,1]` — so the notch at `(0.5,0.5)` and `(0.5,0.95)` is outside
  while the arms `(0.15,0.5)`, `(0.85,0.5)` and the base `(0.5,0.15)` are inside. A
  bounding-box test cannot tell those apart, which is the point of the shape. On-edge
  covers all four edges **and a vertex** (`(0,0)`), the case that breaks a naive ray
  cast, plus a point on a triangle's hypotenuse; outside covers all four sides and a
  far corner; a two-vertex "polygon" is never inside.
- **The centroid is the plan's formula, not the plan's example.** For
  `[100,50,140,150]` on 640×360, `((x1+x2)/2, y2)` normalised is
  `(120/640, 150/360) = (0.1875, 0.41667)`. The plan's test-example line (line 340)
  prints `(0.21875, 0.4167)`, whose x is `x2/640` — the box's right edge, not its
  midpoint. The formula on line 172 is the contract and the example is a slip, so the
  implementation follows the formula and the slip is recorded here and in the test's
  docstring rather than being silently encoded as a constant.
- **Loiter.** Dwell 30 s: nothing at 1000, 1020 or 1029.9, one hit at 1030 with
  `dwell_seconds == 30.0`. Leaving resets it: inside at 1000 and 1010, out at 1015,
  back at 1020, and the hit does not come until 1050 — five seconds after the return,
  not twenty. Two people in one loiter zone are timed separately, and an intrusion
  zone and a loiter zone on the same camera do not share their state.
- **The filters are the camera's floors raised, never lowered**: a zone with
  `min_confidence 0.30` on a camera at `sensitivity 0.45` rejects a 0.35 detection and
  takes a 0.50 one; the same holds for `min_area_ratio`. A zone that named `person`
  ignores a car. A disabled zone never fires. An unknown rule (`flibbertigibbet`) is
  logged **once** across three frames and can never fire.

## Notes

Coordinates are normalized 0..1, matching the server's zone contract.

- **`_frame_size` was being called as a method and was a module function.** Every
  zone test failed with `AttributeError: 'ZoneEvaluator' object has no attribute
  '_frame_size'` on the first run — the unit tests caught a call site the module
  never had. Fixed at the call site; the fallback chain (the frame just inferred on,
  then the payload's `width`/`height`, then the plan's 640×360 tap) is what the
  comment claims.
- **Bottom-center is a decision with a consequence**: a box hanging off the top of the
  frame because someone is close to the camera still counts, because their feet are in
  the zone, and a rule drawn on a driveway should not fire on a head crossing an
  imaginary line halfway up a wall.
- **The dwell state lives on the evaluator**, not in a module-level dict: two cameras
  never share a timer, and a camera whose zones were rewritten gets a fresh evaluator
  from the worker's restart rule (S5-09) instead of inheriting dwell from rules the
  operator deleted.
