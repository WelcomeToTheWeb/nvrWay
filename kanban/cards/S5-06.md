# S5-06 — events.py: open/extend/close state machine + snapshot on open

## Deliverable

Per camera+zone: open (first qualifying hit, emits snapshot JPEG q82 width 640 capped 200 KB) -> extend (hit within 10 s) -> close (10 s idle, or age >= EVENT_MAX_SECONDS). event_key = camera:zone:rule:open_unix.

## Exit criteria

- [x] event_key stable across extends
- [x] 10 s idle close and EVENT_MAX_SECONDS forced close asserted
- [x] open carries exactly one snapshot

## Verification

`cd detector && python3 -m pytest tests/test_events.py -q`

**Observed** 2026-11-03, in `nvr-detector:dev`: `20 passed in 0.15s`.

- **The key is minted once.** `open` at 1000 mints `cam-1:z1:intrusion:1000`; the
  extend at 1005 and the close at 1020 carry the identical string. A hit after the
  close opens `cam-1:z1:intrusion:1030`, a different key, because the server's dedupe
  would otherwise fold a second intrusion into the first event's window.
- **Both closes are asserted.** Idle: open at 1000, extend at 1005, then nothing — no
  close at 1010 (five idle seconds is not a closed event, and `open_count` is still
  1), close at 1015. Age: with `event_max_seconds=20`, the signals at 1020 are
  `[extend, close]` and `open_count` drops to 0, and the next hit at 1025 is an
  `open` under a new key — a post-close `extend` would come back from the server as
  `"error":"event closed"` and mutate nothing (S6-03).
- **Exactly one snapshot.** The open's decodes to a JPEG (`\xff\xd8`) and to the frame
  that triggered, 360×640; the extend's and the close's are `""`. A 1280×720 frame is
  scaled to 640 wide before it is encoded and lands under the 200 KB cap; a 320 px
  frame is not blown up; `SNAPSHOT_QUALITY == 82`.
- **The signal's keys are the plan's twelve, in order**, asserted against
  `PLAN_SIGNAL_KEYS` transcribed from the plan's line 181 rather than imported, and
  the `objects` array is `{"class","conf","box":[x1,y1,x2,y2]}` exactly.
- **The rest of the state machine**: two tracks in one zone are one event whose
  severity is the max of the group and whose `objects` are two; classes accumulate
  across extends without repeating; two zones are two events that close on their own
  schedules; `close_all` (the SIGTERM path) closes both and closing twice does not
  emit twice; a snapshot encoder that raises costs the picture and not the event.

## Notes

ts in the signal is informational: the server clock is authoritative (S6-03).

- **The age ceiling was not closing an event that was still being hit.** The first run
  failed `test_an_event_still_active_past_event_max_seconds_is_closed_anyway`: the
  close loop skipped every key that appeared in this frame's hits, so a person standing
  in the driveway forever was one event forever — the exact case `EVENT_MAX_SECONDS`
  exists for, and a clip nobody assembles. The ceiling is now checked **after** the
  extends, so the sequence at the ceiling is `extend` then `close`, and the next hit
  opens a fresh key. The idle close keeps its own loop for the events nobody hit.
- **The snapshot ladder is quality 82 → 75 → 65 → 55 → 45 and then "send it anyway".**
  A frame that will not compress under the 200 KB cap is sent at the smallest attempt
  with `snapshot is N bytes at quality 45, over the 2048 byte cap; sending it anyway`
  in the log. A blocky picture is still proof; a silently dropped one is a gap in the
  console.
- **The snapshot is encoded once per frame** even when two zones open on it, because
  each event gets the frame that proves it and encoding twice buys a second copy of
  the same JPEG.
- **`ts` is `datetime.fromtimestamp(ts, tz=utc).isoformat()`**, asserted to round-trip
  through `datetime.fromisoformat` with a tzinfo attached — the shape Go's
  `time.RFC3339` parses, which the server then discards.
