**Two decisions this card made, beyond writing tests.**

`deniedActions` in `api/middleware.go` now registers the media verbs ahead of the
routes that will use them — `POST /api/v1/cameras` → `camera.create`,
`PUT/DELETE /api/v1/cameras/:id` → `camera.update` / `camera.delete`,
`POST /api/v1/cameras/:id/probe` → `camera.probe`,
`PUT /api/v1/cameras/:id/zones` → `camera.zones`,
`DELETE /api/v1/events/:id` → `event.delete`. The registry is keyed on the router's
matched path, so the verbs are useful before Step 3 mounts the routes: the criterion
"a `viewer`'s camera write answers 403 and is audited" is assertable **now**, against
the gate the route will run behind, and the suite does exactly that with
`camerasShapedRoute`. The alternative — waiting for Step 3 to test the role gate on
media writes — is how a gate gets tested after it has already been shipped.

`handoffParts` parses the handoff fragment by splitting on `&` and unescaping, not
with `url.ParseQuery`. `ParseQuery` refuses a query containing a semicolon, and
`error_description` carries the operator's IdP's own words verbatim — messages that
are full of semicolons (`… belongs to an existing account; the provider may not link
it by asserting it`). A console reads the fragment with `URLSearchParams`; the suite
now reads it the same way, so a refusal message shaped like a real one cannot break
the test that is supposed to prove the refusal arrives.

The `subject_missing` case is produced by sabotaging the claim where it is published
(`Extra: {"sub": nil}` on the fixture's claims), not by passing an empty subject:
the fixture fills in its own subject when the claims carry none, and a suite that
"tested" an empty subject that the fixture quietly replaced would be asserting that
a door is shut while the door is open.
